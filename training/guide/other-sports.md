# Other sports: how they interact with running and strength

Mainly scheduling and load notes. The shared leg budget and hard-day counting are in
`load-and-progression.md`; substitutions in `periodisation-and-planning.md`.

Labels: **[E]** cited study · **[C]** coaching consensus · **[H]** heuristic.

## Rating a session's leg cost [H]

Rate by what the legs actually did, not the sport label:
- **Easy:** relaxed technique, conversational, no sustained burn. Can sit next to a quality day.
- **Moderate:** steady effort, some hard segments. Not a hard day, but not recovery either.
- **Hard:** intervals, races, games, long climbs, heavy legs next day. Counts as a hard leg day.
If an "easy" session turns out hard, the actual effort decides; adjust the next 1–2 days.

## Ice and inline skating [H]

- Low impact, but quad/glute-dominant in a flexed, partly isometric position; hard sets load the
  same muscles as hills, squats and lunges. Expect heavy quads after intervals or long sessions.
- Easy technique skating ≈ easy aerobic day; it does not build running impact tolerance.
- A hard skate (intervals, speed training, long fast laps) is a hard leg day; it can take the
  place of a running quality session in base/build, less so in the specific phase.
- Avoid heavy lower strength and quality runs the day after a hard skate; consolidate (hard skate
  and strength on the same day, skate first) where the timing allows.
- Inline vs ice: similar muscle demands; inline on rough surfaces adds vibration and fall risk;
  falls matter more in the final 2 weeks before a race.
- Many skate seasons end in spring: don't fill freed time with a sudden running jump.

## Cycling

- Transfers aerobic fitness partly to running, never more than running itself. [E Tanaka 1994]
- Interferes less with strength gains than running when done concurrently. [E Wilson 2012]
- Easy rides are good extra aerobic volume or easy-run substitutes for injury-prone runners;
  they don't train impact tolerance. [H]
- Hard rides (intervals, long climbs, group rides) are hard leg days. Long rides (>2 h) the day
  before a long run reduce long-run quality. [H]
- Rough equivalence for an *easy* session: ~1.5–2 min of easy cycling per 1 min of easy running
  for aerobic time. [H — no good evidence; use session RPE × minutes instead where possible]

## Swimming

- Minimal leg and impact load; swimming transfers least to running VO2max. [E Tanaka 1994]
- Fits on easy days or as active recovery; upper-body and shoulder load matters if the user also
  does pull/press strength or climbing on the same day. [H]
- Deep-water running is the closest no-impact running substitute during injury. [C]

## Climbing and finger strength [H unless marked]

- Upper-body/finger load interferes little with running; schedule freely around runs, but avoid
  hard climbing the day before heavy upper strength.
- Tendons and pulleys adapt slowly; short loading bouts work well: tissue response saturates
  after ~10 min, with ≥6 h before the next bout (engineered-ligament model). [E Baar 2017]
- Finger maintenance in run-focused phases: 1–2 short sessions/wk (e.g. hangboard 5–6 hangs ×
  ~10 s at moderate load, or low-volume limit bouldering). Hangboarding is for climbers with
  ≥1–2 years of experience; pull-ups alone don't maintain climbing-specific finger capacity.
- Stop on finger pain (especially with a pop or swelling) and seek assessment.

## Mobility, handstands and other skill practice [H unless marked]

- Skill practice is **exposure, not load**: short, frequent sessions (10–15 min, 3–6×/wk) work
  better than rare long ones; stop or regress when quality drops.
- Handstand practice loads wrists and shoulders, not legs; it can sit on any day, including easy
  and recovery days. Warm up wrists; don't practise to fatigue before heavy pressing.
- Static stretching doesn't prevent injury [E Lauersen 2014]; long static holds (>60 s per
  muscle) right before fast work can slightly reduce performance, shorter holds within a dynamic
  warm-up have negligible effect. [E Behm 2016] Mobility for range-of-motion goals: any time,
  ideally after training or on easy days.

## Team, court and field sports [H]

Matches and hard training sessions with sprints and changes of direction are hard leg days;
the day after needs easy running or rest. Treat a weekly match as a fixed quality day.
