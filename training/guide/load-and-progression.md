# Load and progression

How fast to build, when to back off, how to restart, and how to count load across sports.
Long-run-specific limits are in `long-runs-and-taper.md`; illness and pain rules in
`recovery-beginners-safety.md`.

Labels: **[E]** cited study · **[C]** coaching consensus · **[H]** heuristic.

## What the evidence says

- **Single-run spikes matter most.** 5,205 runners, 588,000 sessions over 18 months: a run longer
  than the longest of the previous 30 days raised overuse-injury rate. Spike 10–30%: HR 1.64
  (1.31–2.05); 30–100%: HR 1.52; >100%: HR 2.28. Week-to-week distance change showed no
  association; the acute:chronic ratio showed a negative dose-response. [E Frandsen 2025]
- **Two-week jumps >30% may hurt novices.** In 874 novices, overall injury rate didn't differ by
  progression, but >30% over two weeks trended towards more distance-related injuries
  (patellofemoral pain, ITB, MTSS; HR 1.59, 0.96–2.66, not significant). [E Nielsen 2014]
- **The "10% rule" is a default, not a safeguard.** In 532 novices a 13-week plan built on 10%/wk
  did not reduce injuries vs an 8-week plan. [E Buist 2008]
- **Acute:chronic workload ratio (ACWR):** the "sweet spot 0.8–1.3" came from team-sport data
  (Gabbett 2016) and has serious statistical problems (mathematical coupling, ratio artefacts).
  Don't present ACWR as a risk score; at most use it to describe a recent jump. [E Impellizzeri
  2020; Lolli 2019]

## Defaults for progressing running volume [C]

| | New | Some experience | Experienced |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weekly increase | ~5–7% | ~8–10% | ~10–12% |
| Absolute cap | +10 km/wk | +10 km/wk | none |
| Cutback cadence | every 3rd week | every 3rd week | every 4th (3rd if >40 y or injury-prone) |

- Hard ceilings: no single run >1.10× the longest of the last 30 days [E Frandsen]; no >+30% across
  two regular weeks [E Nielsen].
- First week of a plan = current level (average of last 4 weeks), not a jump. [H]
- Progress one thing at a time: volume, long run *or* intensity gets the big step that week. [H]
- Plateau at the peak: hold 2–3 weeks at target rather than climbing to the race. [H]

## Cutback (deload) weeks [C]

- Volume −20–30% (default −25%), long run −25–35%, keep one lighter quality session, strength
  volume down ~⅓ with loads kept.
- After a cutback, resume at the **pre-cutback** level; don't add the skipped increase on top.
- Cadence is a default; move a cutback to line up with a busy work week, travel, illness or a
  tune-up race. [H]

## Returning after a break

Detraining: after stopping completely, VO2max fell ~7% in the first 3 weeks and levelled off
~16% below trained values from ~8 weeks (still above never-trained controls at 12 weeks);
oxidative enzymes halved in ~12 days but stayed ~50% above sedentary levels. [E Coyle 1984]
Highly trained athletes lose ~4–14% VO2max within <4 weeks off. [E Mujika & Padilla 2000] Tendons and bone de-adapt too, so restart below where fitness
"feels" like it is. [H]

| Time off (healthy break) | Restart at | Back to previous load in |
|---|---|---|
| ≤7 days | ~100% (skip intensity first 1–2 runs) | immediately |
| 1–2 weeks | ~70–80% volume, easy | ~1–2 weeks |
| 3–4 weeks | ~50–60% volume, easy only first week | ~3–4 weeks |
| >1–2 months | treat as a new base build | as long as the break or longer |

Table: [H]. Long run follows the spike rule against the last 30 days, not pre-break history.
After illness or injury use the rules in `recovery-beginners-safety.md` first.

## Missed training

Missed sessions are not a backlog: resume at the last completed level, don't double up, don't
cram a missed long run midweek. Details in `periodisation-and-planning.md`. [H]

## Multisport: one shared leg budget

- Running, skating, cycling, field/court sports and lower-body strength all load the same legs;
  the sport label doesn't say how hard it was. [H]
- Count **hard leg days per week** across all sports (quality run, long run, hard skate/ride,
  heavy lower strength, matches). ~3 is a sensible default for amateurs; >4 needs a reason. [H]
- Running causes more interference with strength/hypertrophy gains than cycling in concurrent
  training. [E Wilson 2012] Running also carries impact load that other sports don't replace,
  so non-running cardio keeps aerobic fitness but not running-specific tissue tolerance. [H]
- Session-RPE × minutes (Foster 2001) is a practical common currency to compare total weekly load
  across sports; use it for trends, not as a precise budget. [E Foster 2001]
- When adding a new sport or commitment, hold running volume flat for 2–3 weeks. [H]

## Signals to slow progression [H]

Rising effort at the same pace/HR for >1 week, persistent soreness >48 h, niggles that change
gait, poor sleep, illness, or life stress. Offer a stable or cutback week first. See
`recovery-beginners-safety.md`.
