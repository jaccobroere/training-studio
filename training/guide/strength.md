# Strength: for endurance athletes and in general

Labels: **[E]** cited study · **[C]** coaching consensus · **[H]** heuristic.

## Why (and what not to promise)

- **Running economy:** heavy strength (>80% 1RM), plyometrics and combinations improve running
  economy; heavy loads help most at faster speeds, plyometrics at slower speeds (<~12 km/h).
  [E Llanos-Lagos 2024] Effects need 2–3 sessions/wk for ≥6–14 weeks. [E Blagrove 2018]
  Half-squat 4×4 at max load 3×/wk for 8 weeks improved economy ~5% in well-trained runners. [E Støren 2008]
- **Injury:** strength training cut sports-injury risk (RR ~0.32) and roughly halved overuse injuries
  across sports [E Lauersen 2014]; in runners the pooled effect was not significant except in
  supervised programmes [E Wu 2024]. Say "probably helps if done consistently", not "prevents".
- **Stretching** showed no injury-prevention effect (RR ~0.96). [E Lauersen 2014]

## Dosing basics (general strength)

| Goal | Load / reps | Sets per exercise | Effort | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Learning / novice | 8–12 reps, moderate | 1–3 | RIR 3–4 | 1–2 min |
| Strength | 3–6 reps, ≥80% 1RM | 2–5 | RIR 1–3 | 2–3+ min |
| Hypertrophy | 6–15 reps (≥~30% 1RM works near failure) | ≥10 hard sets/muscle/wk | RIR 0–3 | 1–3 min |
| Power / plyometric | low reps, maximal intent | 2–5 | stop before speed drops | full |

- Frequency 2–3×/wk per muscle group for novices; load increases ~2–10% when the top of the rep
  range is reached at the target RIR. [E ACSM 2009]
- Hypertrophy: ≥10 sets/muscle/wk beats fewer. [E Schoenfeld 2017a] Similar growth across loads
  ~30–85% 1RM when sets are taken close to failure. [E Schoenfeld 2017b]
- RIR (reps in reserve) is a usable autoregulation scale; novices underestimate RIR, so start at
  RIR 3–4. [E Helms 2016]
- Endurance athletes: stay at RIR 1–3, avoid training to failure and big soreness; they need
  strength, not maximal hypertrophy. [H]

## For runners and other endurance athletes [E Blagrove, C]

| Base | 2 | 3×8–10 RIR 3 → 3–4×5–6 RIR 2 | 3×8/leg | 3×12–15 → 3×8 heavy | 40–60 low | 2–3×30–45 s |

## Maintenance

- Strength and muscle size held for up to ~32 weeks with 1 session/wk and 1 set/exercise if
  relative load (intensity) is kept. [E Spiering 2021] In-season, 1 session/wk preserved strength
  gains in cyclists over 13 weeks. [E Rønnestad 2010]
- So in heavy run phases or busy weeks: 1 short heavy session (2–3 lifts, 2 sets, RIR 2–3). [H]

## Placement relative to key sessions

- ≥6 h between a hard endurance session and strength; 0 h separation blunted strength gains,
  24 h best for VO2peak. [E Robineau 2016]
- Put strength on hard-run days, after the run (or later that day), so easy days stay easy. [C]
- No heavy lower-body work the day before a quality run or long run; avoid it within 48 h before
  the long run for new lifters. [C]
- Order within a session: plyometrics/jumps first, then heavy lifts, then accessories. [H]
- Upper-body work interferes little with running; it can go almost anywhere. [H]

## Exercise selection (pick 4–6 per session)

- Squat pattern: back/front/goblet squat, split squat, step-up.
- Hinge: trap-bar/Romanian deadlift, hip thrust, single-leg RDL.
- Calf/soleus: standing (straight-knee) and seated/bent-knee calf raises, loaded.
- Plyometric: pogo hops, skips, bounds, box/drop jumps (progress in that order).
- Trunk/hip: side plank, Copenhagen plank, dead bug, Pallof press.
- Upper (general): push-up/bench, row, overhead press, pull-up.
- Home/no gym: split squats with backpack, single-leg calf raises, Nordic hamstring curls, hip
  thrusts on a sofa, pogo/skip ladders. [H]

## Progression [H unless marked]

- New to lifting: 2–4 weeks at RIR 3–4 with bodyweight/goblet loads; plyometrics only once two
  sessions/wk feel easy.
- Double progression: add reps to the top of the range at target RIR, then add load (~2–10%) and
  return to the bottom. [E ACSM 2009 for load steps]
- Change one variable at a time; rotate exercises every 4–8 weeks at most.

## Writing strength as workout items

One item per exercise: `Exercise — sets × reps @ load or RIR, rest`. Examples:
- `Trap-bar deadlift — 3 × 5 @ RIR 2 (last: 80 kg), 2–3′ rest`
- `Bulgarian split squat — 3 × 8/leg @ RIR 2, 12 kg DBs`
- `Pogo hops — 3 × 15 contacts, full rest`
- `Side plank — 2 × 40 s/side`
Include the last used load when known; otherwise RIR only. Note equipment assumptions.
