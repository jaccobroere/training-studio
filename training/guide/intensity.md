# Intensity: distribution, zones and how to write it

Labels: **[E]** cited study · **[C]** coaching consensus · **[H]** heuristic.

## Distribution

- Well-trained endurance athletes spend ~75–80% of sessions at low intensity (below the first
  threshold) and the rest mostly hard; "80/20" by session count. [E Seiler & Kjerland 2006; Seiler 2010]
- Sub-elite runners: ~80% vs ~67% of time in zone 1 (same ~8% hard) over 5 months → larger
  10.4 km improvement for the low-intensity group (n=12). [E Esteve-Lanao 2007]
- Recreational 10K runners: polarised (~77/3/20% in Z1/Z2/Z3) improved 5.0% vs 3.6% for a
  between-threshold-heavy plan over 10 weeks; not significant overall, larger only in the most
  compliant subset. [E Muñoz 2014]
- Polarised beat threshold-, high-volume- and HIIT-focused blocks in trained athletes over 9 weeks.
  [E Stöggl & Sperlich 2014]
- World-class distance runners train **pyramidal** (Z1 > Z2 > Z3) with lots of threshold work,
  shifting towards polarised near competition. [E Casado 2022; Haugen 2022]
- Practical reading: the shared core is *mostly easy, hard days genuinely hard, few moderate
  "grey" runs*. Pyramidal vs polarised matters less than consistency. [H]

How to apply [C]:
- New runners: ~85–90% easy + strides + one moderate session.
- Others: ~80% easy by time; 1–2 quality sessions/wk (see `running-races.md`).
- Base/build: pyramidal (threshold before VO2max). Specific phase for 5K–half: more polarised
  (race pace + VO2max). Marathon: specific = marathon pace + threshold.
- ≥1 easy/rest day between quality days and between quality and the long run.

## Zones (3-zone model with 5-zone equivalents)

| Zone | Name | RPE (0–10) | Talk test | ~%HRmax | Pace anchor | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 (1–2) | Easy / recovery | 2–3 | full sentences | <~75–80% | ≥60–90 s/km slower than 10K pace | easy runs, long runs |
| Z2 (3) | Marathon pace → threshold | 4–6 | short phrases | ~80–88% | MP to HM pace | MP runs, steady |
| Z2/3 border (3–4) | Threshold | ~7 | a few words | ~88–92% | pace you could race ~60 min | cruise intervals, tempo |
| Z3 (5) | VO2max / 5K pace | 8–9 | can't talk | >~92% | 3–5K pace | 3–5 min reps |
| — | Speed / strides | n/a (short) | — | HR lags | ~mile pace, relaxed | 15–30 s strides, hill sprints |

- Zone borders are the first and second ventilatory/lactate thresholds (VT1/LT1, VT2/LT2). [E Seiler 2010]
- Talk test tracks VT1 well: comfortable speech ≈ below VT1. [E Persinger 2004]
- %HRmax bands and pace anchors in the table: [H]; individual thresholds vary widely.
  Age-predicted HRmax = 208 − 0.7 × age [E Tanaka 2001], but individuals often sit ±10 bpm off
  it [H]; prefer an observed max or a threshold estimate.
- Threshold ≈ pace sustainable for ~60 min, ~83–88% of VO2max. [C Daniels] Lab or field
  thresholds disagree by method; treat any single number as an estimate. [E Jamnick 2020]
- HR drifts upward in long or hot efforts; for long runs, cap by RPE, not by HR alone. [H]

## Pace sources, in order of preference [C/H]

1. Recent race (≤8 weeks, similar terrain) → Daniels VDOT paces.
2. A recent hard time-trial or a run with a threshold-like effort.
3. Goal time → goal paces only; check against 1–2 before training at them.
4. None → RPE + talk test. Always valid; preferred for hills, heat, trail, fatigue.

## Writing intensity in workout items

Give every work segment one primary target plus a fallback, and put the purpose in the note:
- `Warm-up 15′ easy (RPE 2–3)`
- `4 × 8′ @ threshold (RPE 7, ~4:35/km, can say a few words), 2′ jog`
- `Cool-down 10′ easy`
- Easy runs: duration or distance + "RPE 2–3, conversational"; no pace target needed.
- Long runs with a finish: `22 km: first 14 easy, last 8 @ MP (~5:00/km, RPE 5–6)`.
- Strides: `6 × 20 s fast-relaxed, full walk-back recovery` (not intervals; don't count as quality).
- Reps for 5K/10K: distance or time + pace + recovery type (jog/walk) and duration.
- Use time-based reps for beginners and on hills/trails; distance reps on track or flat road. [H]
