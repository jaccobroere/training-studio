# Recovery, beginners and safety

Labels: **[E]** cited study · **[C]** coaching consensus · **[H]** heuristic.
The assistant never diagnoses. It says plainly when something needs a clinician.

## Sleep

- Adults: 7–9 h recommended. [E Hirshkowitz 2015] Athletes benefit from prioritising sleep
  quantity and regularity; naps can top up. [E Walsh 2021]
- Short sleep is associated with higher injury risk in adolescent athletes (<8 h). [E Milewski 2014]
  Treat that as a direction, not a precise threshold for adults. [H]
- A single bad night: keep the plan, maybe soften intensity. Several short nights: swap quality
  for easy, protect sleep before adding load. [H]

## HRV, resting HR and wearables: honest limits

- HRV (lnRMSSD) is most useful as a **7-day rolling average** against the person's own baseline;
  single-day values are noisy. [E Plews 2013]
- HRV-guided training (hard days when HRV is normal/high) matched or modestly beat fixed plans in
  some trials. [E Kiviniemi 2007] Effects are small; don't oversell.
- Resting HR elevated ~5+ bpm above the usual baseline for several mornings, together with poor
  sleep, heavy legs or illness symptoms, is a reason to ease off. [H]
- Do **not** invent a readiness score or treat vendor scores as validated. Describe trends
  ("HRV 7-day average is ~10% below your 2-month baseline") and combine with how the user feels.
  Normal numbers never make pain or illness safe to ignore. [H]
- Overreaching vs overtraining: short-term performance dips that recover in days–weeks are
  normal; persistent decline with mood/sleep disturbance over weeks needs rest and medical
  screening. No single marker diagnoses it. [E Meeusen 2013]

## Energy availability

Chronic under-fuelling (Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport, REDs) impairs health and
performance in all sexes: watch for missed/irregular periods, recurrent bone stress injuries,
unexplained fatigue, illness, falling performance, weight loss. Refer to a doctor/sports
dietitian; don't coach through it. [E Mountjoy 2023]

## Illness [H]

- "Neck check": symptoms only above the neck (runny nose, mild sore throat) → easy session
  allowed, stop if worse. Below the neck (chest, GI), fever, body aches → no training.
- After fever: ≥24–48 h fever-free before easy training; then 2–3 easy days before intensity.
- Return volume roughly ½ → ¾ → full over about as many days as the illness lasted.
- Chest pain, palpitations or unusual breathlessness after a viral illness → stop and see a doctor.

## Pain [H unless marked]

- Pain-monitoring model (from Achilles tendinopathy rehab): pain during activity kept at or below
  ~5/10 (often taught as "below 5") is acceptable **if** it settles by the next morning and doesn't increase week to week; running
  with this rule did not worsen outcomes vs rest. [E Silbernagel 2007] Use only for known, mild,
  tendon-type niggles, not new or sharp pain.
- Stop and get assessed: pain that changes gait, worsens during a run, localised bone pain
  (shin, foot, hip, sacrum) especially with night or rest pain, swelling, or pain lasting >1 week
  despite reduced load.
- Easy cross-training that is pain-free can maintain fitness while a niggle settles.

## Red flags → see a professional promptly

Chest pain or pressure, fainting or near-fainting during exercise, palpitations, unusual
breathlessness, suspected stress fracture, hot/swollen joint, numbness or weakness, dark urine
after very hard efforts, symptoms of heat illness (confusion, stopped sweating), REDs signs.

## Beginners

- Novices get injured ~2× more per hour of running than recreational runners (17.8 vs 7.7 per
  1000 h). [E Videbæk 2015] Their tissues adapt slower than their aerobic fitness.
- Start with **frequency before volume**: 3 sessions/wk of short runs before longer runs. [C]
- Run-walk to start: e.g. NHS Couch to 5K, 9 weeks, 3 sessions/wk, from 1-min run intervals to
  30 min continuous. [C NHS]
- Progress conservatively (see `load-and-progression.md`): a gradual plan alone doesn't remove
  risk. [E Buist 2008]
- Minimum base before a race block: see `running-races.md`. New runners: ≥6 months of consistent
  running before a marathon block. [C]
- All easy at first; add strides after ~4 weeks, one moderate session after ~8 weeks. [H]
- Strength from day one: 2×/wk bodyweight/light loads at RIR 3–4 (`strength.md`).
- Shoes: comfort is a reasonable selection criterion; no shoe type has been shown to prevent
  injury reliably. [H]

## Older athletes [H]

Recovery between hard sessions often takes longer: favour 3-week cutback cadence, 2 hard
running days/wk, keep heavy strength (it matters more with age), extend warm-ups.
