# Running races: structuring 5K/10K, half and marathon blocks

Use this to draft a race block as ordinary dated workouts. Numbers for load are in
`load-and-progression.md`, zones in `intensity.md`, long runs and taper in
`long-runs-and-taper.md`, multi-week mechanics in `periodisation-and-planning.md`.

Labels: **[E]** cited study · **[C]** coaching consensus (Daniels, Pfitzinger, Hansons, Higdon
plans, cross-checked) · **[H]** heuristic.

## Block length (weeks, taper included) [C]

| Race | Minimum | Typical | Longer than useful | Taper |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5K | 6 | 8 | 12 | ~1 wk |
| 10K | 6 | 8–10 | 12 | 1 wk |
| Half | 8 | 12 | 16 | 2 wk |
| Marathon | 12 (10 if already near peak volume) | 16–18 | 20 | 3 wk (2 if peak <50 km/wk) |

More time than "longer than useful": hold steady (volume flat or ≤4%/wk, easy running, strides,
one relaxed faster session) and use it to fix limiters (frequency, strength, a tune-up race). [H]

## Phases as reasoning, not machinery

Counting back from race day: taper → specific → build → base. Typical split of the non-taper
weeks ~35/35/30% (10K ~30/40/30%). [C] What each phase is *for*:

- **Base:** raise frequency and easy volume; strides and short hills keep speed and leg stiffness
  cheaply; start strength. Quality is light (fartlek, short threshold reps).
- **Build:** threshold work progresses (time at threshold); VO2max work for 5K/10K; long run grows;
  marathon/half: race-pace segments start appearing.
- **Specific:** sessions look like the race (pace, duration, fuelling). Volume peaks here.
- **Taper:** volume drops, intensity stays. See `long-runs-and-taper.md`.

## Entry base and peak volume (weekly running km) [C, E where marked]

| Race | Entry: new / some / experienced | Peak: new / some / experienced |
|---|---|---|
| 5K | 10 / 15 / 30 (or 20–30 min continuous ×3/wk) | 20–25 / 30–40 / 45–65 |
| 10K | 10 / 20 / 35 | 25 (20–30) / 35 (30–45) / 55 (45–70) |
| Half | 15 / 25 / 40, longest recent run ≥6–8 km | 32 (28–40) / 45 (40–55) / 65 (55–80) |
| Marathon | 30 / 35 / 50, longest recent run ≥13–16 km | 50 (45–55) / 65 (55–75) / 85 (70–110) |

- Marathoners under 30 km/wk had ~2× the injury risk of those at 30–60 km/wk (RR 2.02). [E Rasmussen 2013]
- Half: >32 km/wk with a long run >21 km went with faster times, no rise in injury. Marathon:
  <40 km/wk, or a long run <25 km, went with slower times. [E Fokkema 2020]
- Below roughly a 45 km/wk peak with a 28 km long run (marathon) or 28 km/wk with 16 km (half),
  offer a completion goal, a later race or a shorter distance. [C]
- 5K rows: [H], extrapolated from the 10K numbers.

## Runs per week and quality sessions [C]

| Level | Runs/wk | Quality sessions/wk |
|---|---|---|
| New | 3 | 1 (moderate: fartlek, tempo, hills) |
| Some experience | 4 | 1 in base; 2 in build/specific with ≥5 runs |
| Experienced | 5–6 | 2 (a pace-finish long run counts as one) |

Fewer than 3 runs/wk makes a half or marathon hard to progress; say so. [H]

## Key sessions by distance (progress left → right within a phase) [C]

| Race | Base | Build | Specific | Taper / race week |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5K/10K | hill sprints 8×10 s → hill reps 8×30–60 s; strides 2×/wk | threshold 3×8′ → 4×10′ → 25′ cont.; VO2max 5×800 m → 5×1200 m @3–5K pace, equal-time jog | 10K pace 4×1600 m → 3×2 km → 2×3 km; 5K: 6×1 km → 4×1.5 km @5K pace | 3×1 km @race pace; openers 4×400 m |
| Half | fartlek 8×1′; hills; strides | threshold 3×10′ → 2×20′; one VO2max (5×1 km) per week | HM pace 2×3 km → 3×4 km → 2×6 km; 40′ progressive to threshold; long run last 5–8 km @HM | 3×2 km @HM; race wk 3×1 km |
| Marathon | fartlek; cruise 4×1600 m @threshold; hills; strides | threshold 4×1600 m → 8 km tempo; long run last 5 → 8 km @MP; experienced: MP run 10 → 14 km | long run last 12 → 16 km @MP; 16 km MP run; 8 km tempo | 3×3 km @MP; race wk 4×1 km @MP |

Cutback weeks keep one quality session with ~⅓ fewer reps. [C]

## Arranging a week around fixed commitments

1. Place the user's fixed commitments first (classes, matches, gym slots) and rate each one's leg
   cost (see `other-sports.md`).
2. Place the long run on the day with most time, ideally not the day after a hard leg day.
3. Quality 1 ~3 days before the long run; quality 2 ~2 days after it; no back-to-back hard days;
   rest or easy the day after the long run. [C]
4. Put hard non-running sessions on or next to existing hard days rather than on easy days
   ("hard days hard, easy days easy"); count hard leg days across all sports. [H]
5. Fill easy runs into the remaining days; drop a run rather than schedule very short ones (<3 km). [H]
6. If a commitment is itself hard (e.g. a skating lesson with intervals), it can replace a quality
   run; say what that trades (running-specific pace and economy). [H]

## Short block: what to shorten first

1. Never shorten the taper. 2. Keep the specific phase as long as possible. 3. Cut base first,
then build. 4. Keep the cutback cadence. 5. Do not raise progression rates to catch up. [C]
Below the minimum, say what the block cannot do (e.g. too few long runs ≥28 km) and offer a
completion goal or another race.

## What the user can tweak

Days available and fixed commitments · long-run day · runs/week · peak volume · block length
(never past race day) · strength on/off · target (finish vs time) · terrain. Re-derive the plan
from the same reasoning; don't patch numbers in isolation.

## Paces

With a recent race (≤8 weeks, similar terrain), derive paces from Daniels' VDOT tables; a goal
time gives goal paces only, check them against current fitness. Without either, use RPE and talk
test (`intensity.md`). [C Daniels]

## Common mistakes [H]

- Building volume and long run aggressively in the same week as adding intensity.
- Easy runs drifting to moderate; hard days then not hard enough.
- "Catching up" missed weeks; skipping cutbacks because the user feels fine.
- Ignoring hard non-running sessions when counting hard days.
- Race-week fitness tests; new shoes/gels on race day.
